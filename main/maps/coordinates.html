MAPS & PLACES / POSITION

Latitude and longitude,
clearly.

A coordinate looks precise only when you also know its reference system, order, datum, and precision.

THE MAIN IDEA

Latitude runs north–south; longitude wraps east–west.

Latitude measures angular distance north or south of the equator, whose latitude is zero degrees. Lines of latitude are parallels: they circle the globe east–west and remain parallel to each other. Longitude measures angular distance east or west of a chosen prime meridian, commonly the one passing through Greenwich. Lines of longitude meet at the poles and divide the globe into east–west positions. A position written in degrees can also include minutes and seconds, or a decimal fraction of a degree. One minute of latitude is approximately one nautical mile, while a minute of longitude becomes progressively shorter toward the poles because the meridians converge. Many geographic data formats write latitude first and longitude second, but some mapping and programming interfaces use the reverse order. Always read the labels rather than relying on a memorized sequence. Negative decimal latitude often indicates south and negative longitude west, but conventions and display formats vary. A coordinate pair is incomplete without a datum or reference frame in applications requiring precise alignment; the Earth is not a perfect sphere, and different reference systems model it differently. More digits do not automatically mean more accuracy. A phone may display many decimal places while its measured position is uncertain by several meters or more, depending on satellite geometry, buildings, atmosphere, and device conditions. A coordinate can point to a private property, a hazardous location, or an ecologically sensitive site. Do not share precise locations of people, nests, or protected species without permission. When reading a coordinate, check the hemisphere, the number format, the coordinate reference system, and the accuracy estimate. For an ordinary trip, a reputable navigation service can convert a location into an appropriate display, but local access restrictions and on-the-ground conditions remain important. For surveying, boundary disputes, scientific research, aviation, emergency response, or engineering, use official reference data and professionals qualified for the task. A coordinate is a carefully defined address on a model of Earth; it is not, by itself, proof that a person or feature is exactly where the digits suggest.

TRY THIS

Check which number is latitude.

Look up a well-known public landmark using a reputable map. Record whether the service lists latitude first and note the hemisphere or sign. Compare the coordinates shown in a second service, noticing rounding and datum assumptions rather than expecting every displayed digit to match.

Privacy: Avoid sharing sensitive personal or wildlife coordinates. Navigation accuracy varies by place and device.
