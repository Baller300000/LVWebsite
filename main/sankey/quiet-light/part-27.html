Nicholas didn’t act impulsively. So when he brought it up, it was after thinking it through. “I got something,” he said, standing just inside Alex’s room, a small paper bag in his hand. Alex looked up from his bed, curious. “That sounds suspicious.” “It’s not,” Nicholas replied. Then, after a beat: “I think.” Alex smiled faintly. “That’s not reassuring.” Nicholas walked over and set the bag down between them. “I wasn’t sure if this was… appropriate,” he said. “So if it isn’t, we don’t use it.” Alex’s expression shifted—attention sharpening, but not tense. “Okay,” he said quietly. Nicholas opened the bag. Inside: a simple, soft tail attachment. Subtle. Not exaggerated. And a small box of dog treats—plain, human-edible biscuits, nothing unsafe or demeaning. Alex stared at them for a moment. Not shocked. Not embarrassed. Just… processing. “You thought about this,” he said. Nicholas nodded once. “Yes.” “Why?” Nicholas didn’t hesitate. “Because you told me what helps you feel… aligned. And I want to support that. If it’s still something you want.” Silence. Then Alex reached out, picking up the tail, running his fingers over the material. It was soft. Carefully chosen. “You didn’t go overboard,” he said quietly. “That was intentional.” Alex looked at him again, something warmer in his expression now. “Yeah,” he said. “I want this.”
