They stopped meeting. Not intentionally. Not formally. But the rhythm broke. Days passed. Then weeks. Reece still sat by the fountain. But he didn’t draw as much. He replayed the conversation over and over. He hadn’t meant to imply anything. He hadn’t meant to suggest Alex was inconsistent or fake or— But maybe he had. Maybe that’s what he saw. Different versions. Different responses. Different expressions depending on who he was with. Was that… wrong? Reece didn’t know. He only knew he missed him. Alex avoided the courtyard. Not because of Reece. Because of what Reece had almost said. Because it hit too close to something he already feared. You’re inconsistent. You’re too much. You’re not real. He had built himself carefully. Piece by piece. Choosing what to show, when to show it, how to survive the constant evaluation of others. And Reece—quiet, observant Reece—had seen the cracks. That should have meant trust. Instead, it felt like exposure.
