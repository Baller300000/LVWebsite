Alex, for his part, began to linger longer. Reece didn’t fill silence. That was rare. Most people rushed to occupy every empty space, as if silence was something dangerous. With Reece, silence felt… structured. Safe. But safety had edges. Alex knew that. One afternoon, the air shifted colder, wind cutting through the courtyard. Alex sat beside Reece on the stone wall instead of standing. “You ever get tired,” Alex asked, “of feeling like you’re waiting for something?” Reece didn’t answer immediately. He rarely did. “Yeah,” he said finally. “But I don’t know what I’m waiting for.” Alex nodded. “Same.” He traced a line along the stone with his finger. “Sometimes I think I already missed it.” Reece glanced at him. “Missed what?” Alex smiled faintly. “The version of me people would’ve accepted easier.” Reece frowned slightly. “You seem… accepted.” Alex let out a quiet breath. Not quite a laugh. “That’s because I worked for it.” He looked ahead, not at Reece. “Confidence is expensive.” Reece didn’t know how to respond. So he said the only thing he could. “You don’t seem fake.” Alex turned then. Something in his expression shifted—softened, but also… tightened. “That’s a dangerous compliment,” he said. “Why?” “Because it makes me want to believe it.”
