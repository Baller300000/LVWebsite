A NOTE BEFORE COOKING

Understanding is an ingredient too.

Recipes are practical instructions; these pages explore the reasoning behind some of them. Small experiments can teach a lot, but kitchens involve heat, sharp tools, allergens, raw ingredients, and food that must be handled safely. Keep experiments modest, identify ingredients, follow proven food-safety guidance, and use an adult's help when appropriate. Measurements, equipment, and water conditions vary, so no single explanation promises identical results in every kitchen. When a dish fails, you have data: record what changed before declaring the method mysterious. Treat storage times and temperatures as questions for current public health guidance, not internet folklore. These guides build intuition, never substitute for tested preservation instructions.
