CREATIVE WORKSHOP / PUBLICATION

A tiny zine is
a complete world.

A handmade booklet can have a clear idea and satisfying sequence without sophisticated equipment.

THE MAIN IDEA

Sequence is the secret power of a small publication.

A zine is an independently made small publication that can use writing, drawing, photography, collage, or any combination suited to its creator. Its scale encourages clear choices: a handful of pages must make an invitation, develop an idea, and offer a landing place. Start with a subject you want to share rather than an obligation to fill every page. Give the publication a working title and describe the intended reader in concrete terms. A one-sheet folded zine can be made from one printed page, but its panels emerge in a non-obvious reading order. Make a cheap blank mock-up first, number the panels, and flip through it before designing digitally. Print a dummy, cut and fold it exactly as the final copy will be folded, then check whether text is upside down, hidden inside a fold, or impossible to reach. Margins, paper size, printer bleed, duplex settings, and fold accuracy all affect the result. A print test is part of design, not a sign you should have known better. Choose type sizes that remain readable after printing at actual scale; rich colors may render differently on inexpensive printers. A zine can be black-and-white and still feel deliberate when image, rhythm, and language work together. If you include photographs, music, illustrations, or quotations made by others, check the rights and permission required for your intended print run and distribution. Crediting an author does not automatically grant permission to reproduce copyrighted work. Personal stories about other people also deserve consent and careful redaction of identifying details. Share copies in places where distribution is welcome, state any price or license clearly, and consider whether the subject might put an interviewee or the author at risk. Leaving a blank page can make room for reflection; it is not automatically a wasted page. Sequence is shaped by contrast: a dense page followed by a nearly quiet page changes pace. Ask a reader to handle the mock-up without help. If they turn a panel in the wrong direction, fix the physical object rather than blaming the reader. The final zine need not be perfect. Its appeal often lies in the intimate scale, visible decisions, and evidence that one person cared enough to make an idea shareable.

TRY THIS

Make and number one paper dummy.

Fold a blank sheet into the format you intend to use, then number every panel in reading order. Add a short title and one sentence to each planned page. Unfold it to inspect sequence, and test a low-cost print before sharing the finished work.

Rights and privacy: Obtain necessary permission for reused material and avoid exposing another person's sensitive details.
